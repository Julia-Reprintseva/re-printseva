What people come with

Three situations where AI “doesn’t work”

All three happened in this project. The solutions are not advice — they are what was actually done for sixteen months.

“I need an overview of a topic, and ChatGPT gives me beautiful fluff: generalities, not a single source.”

The model answers a question it wasn’t asked: it retells what it “knows” instead of searching.

SolutionThe task becomes a four-page brief: the model’s role, scope, research questions, mandatory methodology, a card format with fields, starting sources. After such a brief the model works as a cataloguer, not an essayist. How the brief is built →
“I can’t tell whether it’s lying. Everything looks convincing.”

Links to pages that don’t exist, quotes nobody wrote, dates “approximately”. A beautiful answer is indistinguishable from a correct one.

SolutionEvery claim gets a confidence level; strong claims are checked against scans and primary sources; a second system gives an independent view; fabrications are logged, not hushed up. 241 fabrications caught →
“I have forty scanned PDFs and it simply won’t take them.”

Uploads fail silently, limits are documented nowhere, files are “too large” without explanation.

SolutionThe engineering is mine: OCR, compression and slicing to fit the limits, knowledge bases of dozens of editions, custom assistants. The hidden 80 MB per-account limit was found and described exactly this way. The limit story →

Method

I don’t write “magic prompts”. I write terms of reference

goal→inputs→quality criteria→output format→checks and verification

The prompt is built from the model’s own requirements (OpenAI and Google documentation), not from pretty phrases. Given such a brief, GPT-5 starts proposing missing checks on its own: “filter out post-Soviet reconstructions”, “give an estimated date with a caveat”. You cannot get that by writing “be careful”.

01 · TASK

Three lines from the client

“Analyse the Moscow Metro inscriptions of 1935–1953. Split them into groups: façades, platforms, wayfinding, memorial…” The rest is my job.

02 · SCAFFOLD

Periodisation and a map of sources

A quick pass in Perplexity: periods, object types, corpora — books, archives, museum databases. Ten minutes after the brief there is a skeleton.

03 · BRIEF

A four-page prompt

The client states the task in one sentence — I interview them: call, write, ask again. The draft goes into my ChatGPT project built specifically for writing prompts by OpenAI’s documentation: the model asks clarifying questions — what the brief still lacks — and we answer them together with the client on a call or in the chat. The result: the model’s role, scope, research questions, mandatory methodology, a card format with fixed fields, starting sources.

04 · RUN

Several systems in parallel

ChatGPT Deep Research for synthesis, Perplexity for links, Gemini for a second view; when the topic deserves it — all seven. One system’s output becomes another’s input.

05 · CHECK

Against primary sources, with a confidence level

Scanned tables of contents, library page viewers, book PDFs. A beautiful answer is not a fact: every claim is marked high, medium or low confidence.

06 · DELIVERY

Base, report, summary — and an open debrief

Google Docs, PDF, a comparative table of conclusions. The client’s notes are closed the same day. Then a post on what worked and what didn’t.

Тема

Монументальная пропаганда и массовое праздничное оформление в СССР (1917–1991) — визуальные носители, типографика, лозунги, материалы, корпуса источников.

Роль модели

Ты — исследователь визуальной культуры СССР и архивный аналитик. Твоя цель — собрать, проверить и систематизировать максимум визуальных источников (фото, схем, публикаций, разворотов, каталогов), а не пересказать общие знания. Работай как автор научного каталога и исследовательского досье.

Цель

  1. Построить исследовательский каркас темы (периодизация, типы объектов, корпуса).
  2. Собрать и описать ядро визуальных источников по каждому периоду и типу объекта.
  3. Систематизировать типографику + материалы + формулы лозунгов.
  4. Выдать результат в форме, пригодной для дальнейшей работы: каталог карточек объектов + «карта корпусов» + выводы.

Границы и фокус

География: СССР (центры + регионы + республики). Объекты: временные праздничные конструкции; стационарные объекты (памятники, панно, мозаики, надписи в архитектуре, световые конструкции); метро, вокзалы, стадионы, ДК, НИИ как типовые носители. Релевантно всё, где текст/буква/лозунг — часть визуальной композиции.

Исследовательские вопросыответь на каждый

  1. Какие типы носителей доминируют в каждом периоде и почему?
  2. Как меняется язык лозунгов: императивы → описательные формулы → «утопическое завтра» → кризис формы?
  3. Как меняется типографика: рукописное → конструктивистские гротески → стандартизация (ОСТ/ГОСТ) → функциональные гротески → буквы в архитектуре?
  4. Какие корпуса дают максимум материала и как к ним «присасываться»?
  5. Какие узлы дают наиболее плотные примеры для исследователя шрифтов?

Обязательная методология

  1. Каркас: периоды 1917–1922; 1920-е–1932; конец 1930-х–1953; 1953–1960-е; 1970–1980-е; конец 1980-х–1991. Для каждого — темы, лозунги, типографика, материалы, типы объектов.
  2. «Карта корпусов» — не менее 20–30 позиций: что искать, по каким ключам.
  3. «Каталог карточек» — минимум 40, лучше 80+; по 8–12 на период.
  4. Не додумывай датировки: нет точной даты — помечай как приблизительную и объясняй.
  5. Синтез: закономерности, переломные точки, где копать дальше.

Формат результатастрого

  1. A) Executive summary (1–1,5 стр.)
  2. B) Периодизация: 6 блоков на период
  3. C) Карта корпусов
  4. D) Каталог карточек — единый формат: ID · период · тип объекта · год и уровень уверенности · место · событие · носитель/материал · текст лозунга · типографика · визуальное описание · автор · источник (точная ссылка) · заметки
  5. E) Приложение «как добивать визуал»

Критерии качества

Максимум первичных источников. Каждый сильный тезис — со ссылкой. Чётко отделяй факт / интерпретацию / гипотезу. Никаких «красивых обобщений» без опоры на корпус.

Стартовые источникиобязательно использовать и расширить

imwerden: «Агитационно-массовое искусство… 1917–1932» · tehne: «Декоративное искусство СССР» · russiainphoto.ru · корпуса по мозаикам, метро, декрету 1918.

My role

AI research architect

Not a co-author: the text is the author’s. Not a “prompt operator”: AI here is a tool. In between is the person who turns a topic into a brief, a brief into a process, and the output of that process into verified material you can build on.

Yuri Gordon put it shorter: “neuro-captain”, “head of the neuro-assistants”.

What this means for your project

You remain the author of the decision, the text or the product. I take everything between the question and the verified answer: framing the task, the route through the tools, checking and assembling the material. The line of authorship is agreed up front and kept — as it was here.

And if there is nobody to write the text — I am a journalist by first profession and can write it myself, on verified material. Here Yuri Gordon wrote himself because that is how his project was set up, not because it has to be that way.

Catching the AI lying

For the Paris map DeepSeek produced two quotes for each of ninety places — from Proust to Gainsbourg, with dates and references to the National Library. The same reference recurred for dozens of different authors. Hence the rule: no quote goes to work without checking the text.

80 MB

The hidden limit ChatGPT doesn’t mention

A week of uploads kept failing without explanation. It turned out the file-volume limit applies not to a project but to the whole account — and shows no error. I found it, described it publicly, worked out a way around it — and the 18-edition base has worked ever since.

Four steps of verification

The “Art of Type” albums: the first answer looked convincing — with percentages and lists. A second pass through the library’s page scans showed that some contributors were invented and the percentages made up. Third — from the scanned tables of contents. Fourth — a base of 97 names with life dates. The difference between “looks true” and “true” is three extra hours.

Questions

What people usually ask before the call

How long does it take?

Reconnaissance — one working day: this project has a cycle where the task arrived at 11:55 and the client had the final PDF at 14:01. A full study — one to two weeks, depending on the size of the corpus and the depth of checking. Support — at the rhythm of your project.

What do I get at the end?

A document answering the questions of the brief; a base or card catalogue with sources and a confidence level for each; a comparative digest if several systems were used; the briefs and prompts themselves. All of it stays with you and can be reused.

What if my topic isn’t history or culture?

The method doesn’t depend on the topic: a market, a regulatory base, competitors, a counterparty check. The corpus of sources and the checking criteria change; the pipeline stays. History and culture are the hardest domain for AI — sources are rare and it lies there most readily. If the method survived Soviet typefaces, it will survive your market.

Who owns the result?

The right to use the result is yours: the reports, bases and prompts stay in your project and keep working without me. Authorship of the research materials themselves is mine, as with any author of a text or a database; that doesn’t stop you using them, but it means the signature under the work doesn’t disappear. As in this case: the book text is the author’s, the research is mine, and both are visible. The boundaries are agreed up front, in writing.

Which systems do you use?

Whichever fit the task: in this project it was ChatGPT (Deep Research, custom projects), Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek, Grok, Qwen, ChatPDF, Gamma. The list is open — tools change every few months, the method doesn’t. Several systems often run in parallel and are compared on one brief, as in the six-systems story.

Can I see what it looks like?

Yes, everything is open: thirteen reports and their briefs on the research pages, the quote book of fabrications, the almanac of chapters. Showing the client the whole work is part of the method.
